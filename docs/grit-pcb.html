Grit Board Layer

What sits around the 26-chip machine on the PCB: power entry, the run/single-step clock, the reset button, bank switches, thirty debug LEDs behind buffers, and logic-analyzer headers. All of it is in the board file boards/grit/netlist.json with pins, packages and LCSC numbers where settled; the simulator sees every part here as passive and drives CLK at the multiplexer's output. Full text in docs/grit-pcb.md.

1. Ground rules

  1. Assembly. JLCPCB places everything SMD, and its through-hole service places the DIP sockets, the 0.1″ headers and the DB9. If the DB9 is refused it is the one hand-soldered part. Jake supplies and programs the GALs, flash and SRAM; the board carries only their sockets.
  2. Sockets. GALs in DIP-24 300 mil sockets (the ATF22V10C is the narrow DIP); flash DIP-32 600 mil; SRAM DIP-28 600 mil.
  3. Power. 5 V wall adapter on a 5.5/2.1 mm centre-positive barrel jack, 1.1 A polyfuse, P-MOSFET against reverse polarity, 100 µF at the jack, four 10 µF across the board, 100 nF at every chip, a power LED. No regulator. The MAX811L resets the machine below 4.63 V rather than letting it wander.
  4. One name per net; pins are chip.function; active-low nets end in _n.

3. Debug LEDs

Thirty 0603 LEDs with 1k, behind four 74HC541 buffers, so no logic net carries more than one CMOS input. Each LED shows its pin's level; the three active-low nets have their LEDs hung from VCC so they light when the strobe or reset is active.

The IR and STEP LEDs read the opcode and the step in binary; the control-line LEDs read the microword as the pipeline register holds it.

5. Bank switches, reset, serial

Two 4-position DIP switches with 10k pull-ups; ON pulls a line low. swb0 is the program flash bank (A15..A18, 16 banks of 32 K words); swb1 the microcode bank (A10..A12, 8 images, one position spare). All ON is bank 0, which is what the simulation assumes. 10k pull-downs hold Addr14 and Addr15 at “flash” while nothing drives the bus.

The reset button sits on the MAX811L's MR#, which the part pulls up and debounces. The DB9 is female and wired as a DCE, so a straight cable reaches a PC or USB-serial adapter; the UART runs on the stocked 14.7456 MHz crystal, divisor 8 for 115200.

6. Layout rules

  1. CLK skew under 2 ns between any two GALs. The GAL's minimum clock-to-output is 2 ns; more skew than that is a hold violation on the PC's self-addressed load (the one timing rule the model found). About 300 mm of FR4, so: one CLK net from umux0, no chip more than about 150 mm further from it than any other, never daisy-chained across the board and back.
  2. Nothing else is length-sensitive: every other margin is 100 ns or more at 250 ns a clock.
  3. 100 nF within a few millimetres of each VCC pin; the 10 µF parts one per region.
  4. Sockets, headers and LEDs on top; single-sided SMD if it fits, since the debug side is the side you look at.
  5. Silk: LED names, the active-low marks on the three VCC-hung LEDs, pin 1 and pin names on the headers, ON = 0 on the bank switches, RUN / STEP on the slide switch, centre-positive at the jack.
