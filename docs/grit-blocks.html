Grit Block Diagram

Microcoded: every flash word is a horizontal microinstruction, one bit per control line, executed in a fetch clock and an execute clock. 12 GALs, 20 chips. Blue is the data bus, gold the address bus, green point-to-point. Each block lists its own pins; every control line comes from the microinstruction register, and the four timing signals from the glue chip.

The microword: one bit per control line

BitSignalIn the execute clock
15LITthe next word is data: the PC keeps the address bus, the flash drives D, the PC steps past the word
14MEMRDmemory at A drives D
13ALUOEthe ALU drives D
12WRmemory at A takes D, a quarter-clock pulse
11ALDA loads D at the end of the clock
10BLDB loads D
9PCLDthe PC loads D
8PCEQthe PC loads D if A equals B
7:6F1 F0ALU function: 00 add, 01 and, 10 nor, 11 pass B
5:0unused

The assembler refuses a word with more than one of LIT, MEMRD, ALUOE, or with MEMRD and WR together.

The two clocks of a microop

fetch    A drives the address bus for the first half, the PC for the second; the flash drives D;
         at the edge: MIR := word[15:6], PC += 2, EXEC := 1
execute  A drives the address bus (the PC, if LIT); MEMRD, ALUOE or LIT picks the driver of D;
         WE# pulses in quarter two if WR; at the edge: ALD/BLD/PCLD/PCEQ load, PC += 2 if LIT,
         MIR clears, EXEC := 0

Loading an immediate

0x8400   LIT BLD                 ; execute: flash[PC] -> D -> B
0x1234                           ; the literal, skipped by the PC
0x8800   LIT ALD                 ; the same aimed at A: A = &r5
0x800A
