Grit Block Diagram

A microprogrammed machine: a small instruction set in the program flash, a microcode ROM in the other flash pair, a step counter, and a pipeline register that latches one microword per clock. 13 GALs, 23 chips. Blue is the data bus D, gold the address bus Addr, purple the microcode ROM's private wires, green point-to-point. Every control line is a pin of the pipeline register and changes only at a clock edge.

The microword: one bit per control line

BitLineFor the whole clock
0PCDRVthe PC drives Addr; otherwise A drives Addr
1MEMRDthe memory selected by Addr drives D: flash, SRAM or UART, by Addr's top bits
2ALUOEthe ALU drives D
3WEthe memory selected by Addr takes D
4ALDA copies D at the ending edge
5BLDB copies D at the ending edge
6PCLDthe PC copies D at the ending edge
7PCINCPC += 2 at the ending edge
8IRLDIR copies D[15:11] and the step counter clears at the ending edge: the fetch
9, 10F1 F0ALU function: 00 add, 01 and, 10 nor, 11 pass B
11..15spare: debug outputs, a halt flag

Two rules shape every sequence: the driver of D never changes between consecutive words (an idle word in between lets the old driver float for a whole clock), and a MEMRD or WE word is followed by a word with the same Addr driver, with ALUOE kept after WE, so every hold time is a clock long. The word after a fetch is always a nop because the ROM's word for the next step is already in flight when the IR changes.

Loading an immediate: LDA 0x1234

program:   0x0800   LDA imm (opcode 1 in bits 15:11)
           0x1234

clock  step  microword                 what happens at the ending edge
  1     0    PCINC                     PC -> the immediate
  2     1    PCDRV MEMRD ALD           flash[PC] on D; A := 0x1234
  3     2    PCINC                     PC -> the next instruction
  4     3    PCDRV MEMRD IRLD          flash[PC] on D; IR := its opcode, step := 0
  5     4    nop                       the pipeline slot; the ROM is already presenting the next opcode's step 0
